\# Protocol Qv Rev 7.1 — Adversarial Findings and Accountability Boundaries



\*\*Date:\*\* December 2025  

\*\*Status:\*\* Interpretive / Non-Normative  

\*\*Scope:\*\* Post-Incident AI Accountability  

\*\*Authority:\*\* Protocol Qv Rev 7.1 (unchanged)  

\*\*Purpose:\*\* Documentation of adversarial stress testing, gap classification, and operating boundaries



---



\## 1. Executive Abstract



This document records the outcomes of an adversarial stress test applied to \*\*Protocol Qv Rev 7.1\*\*, an evidence-first post-incident AI accountability protocol. The objective of the exercise was failure discovery rather than validation: to identify where accountability reconstruction breaks down under realistic institutional, legal, and geopolitical constraints.



The findings reaffirm that Protocol Qv Rev 7.1 remains internally coherent and directionally correct, while also clarifying its operating boundaries. Several gaps were confirmed as hardenable through engineering or procedural artifacts. Other failure modes were determined to be terminal conditions that must be declared rather than solved. A third class of limitations—power constraints—were identified as conditions that must be exposed and documented, not engineered away.



This document is descriptive. It introduces no new requirements, makes no future commitments, and does not alter the doctrine of Protocol Qv Rev 7.1.



---



\## 2. Protocol Overview (Descriptive Only)



Protocol Qv is a post-incident accountability protocol for AI systems. It activates after deployment, when an incident, deviation, or credible harm signal has occurred, and governs how explanations are reconstructed and preserved.



The protocol specifies:

\- How incident boundaries are defined and frozen

\- How evidence is preserved and classified

\- How human interventions are recorded and attributed

\- How uncertainty and unknowns are protected

\- How regulators are provided verifiable facts instead of narratives



Protocol Qv does not optimize models, predict harm, or guarantee safety. Its objective is to make post-incident accountability reconstructable, auditable, and resistant to retrospective narrative manipulation.

\## 3. Gap Register Summary (GAP 1–14)



The following gaps are acknowledged under Protocol Qv Rev 7.1. Their existence does not invalidate the protocol; they define its current maturity.



\### Original Gaps (1–10)



\- \*\*GAP 1:\*\* Formal incident boundary freezing artifact  

\- \*\*GAP 2:\*\* Evidence classification schema (observed, inferred, unknown)  

\- \*\*GAP 3:\*\* Counterfactual typing and rejection rules  

\- \*\*GAP 4:\*\* Responsibility mapping artifact (responsibility lattice)  

\- \*\*GAP 5:\*\* Unified canonical incident record  

\- \*\*GAP 6:\*\* Unknowns register as a first-class artifact  

\- \*\*GAP 7:\*\* Incident severity taxonomy  

\- \*\*GAP 8:\*\* Regulator handoff / incident evidence pack generator  

\- \*\*GAP 9:\*\* Cross-incident comparability index  

\- \*\*GAP 10:\*\* Public verifiability and third-party replay path  



\### EU AI Act–Derived Gaps (11–14)



\- \*\*GAP 11:\*\* Declared purpose and use-envelope lock  

\- \*\*GAP 12:\*\* Regulatory notification clock anchoring  

\- \*\*GAP 13:\*\* Tiered reporting deadlines  

\- \*\*GAP 14:\*\* Regulator template conformance  



All gaps are tracked explicitly. No gap is assumed resolved unless closed by an enforceable artifact.

\## 4. Adversarial Stress Testing Method



The stress test was conducted by subjecting Protocol Qv Rev 7.1 to adversarial reasoning scenarios intended to simulate real-world pressure rather than idealized compliance conditions.



The focus was on:

\- Institutional resistance to disclosure

\- Legal and jurisdictional conflicts

\- Delayed or aggregated harm manifestation

\- Disputes over activation legitimacy

\- Loss or compromise of cryptographic trust anchors



The method emphasized boundary discovery: identifying where the protocol’s guarantees cease to hold and where explicit refusal or declaration is required.



---



\## 5. Findings Classification



\### Hardenable Gaps



These are deficiencies that can be addressed through additional artifacts or procedural controls without changing the doctrine:



\- Aggregated or slow-motion systemic harm activation

\- Recording of activation disputes (assertion, challenge, refusal)

\- Certain identity continuity and intervention overload scenarios



These findings inform future hardening work but do not alter Rev 7.1.



\### Boundary / Terminal Conditions



These are conditions under which accountability reconstruction cannot proceed, regardless of engineering effort:



\- Sovereign seizure or national security override

\- Sanctions-driven deadlock

\- Non-cooperative states or vendors

\- Jurisdictional legal impossibility



These conditions must be declared explicitly when encountered. They are not solvable within the protocol.



\### Power Constraints



These are situations where accountability is blocked by structural power, not technical failure:



\- “Too big to fail” economic overrides

\- Regulator incapacity or capture

\- Evidence access blocked by law or force



Protocol Qv treats these as exposures to be documented, not gaps to be closed.



---



\## 6. Operating Envelope



Protocol Qv Rev 7.1 operates within a defined envelope:



\- It guarantees preservation and traceability of evidence \*\*until\*\* accountability is blocked.

\- It does not guarantee that accountability will always be achieved.

\- It requires explicit declaration when reconstruction cannot proceed due to external constraints.



Success is defined as making the prevention of accountability visible, timestamped, and auditable.



---



\## 7. Implications for Future Revisions



The findings clarify, but do not expand, the scope of future work. Any subsequent revision would need to:



\- Close explicitly accepted hardenable gaps

\- Preserve clear declarations of terminal conditions

\- Avoid claims that the protocol can override sovereignty, law, or institutional power



No future revision is assumed or promised by this document.



---



\## 8. Conclusion



Protocol Qv Rev 7.1 remains a valid and disciplined post-incident accountability framework. The adversarial stress test strengthened its credibility by clarifying where its guarantees hold and where they must stop.



By refusing to claim universal accountability and instead documenting where accountability is prevented, the protocol maintains integrity under real-world constraints. This document records that boundary honestly and without normative expansion.
