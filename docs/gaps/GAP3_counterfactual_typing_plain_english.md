1\. What was attempted



To prevent speculative or blame-shifting explanations after an incident by forcing all counterfactuals to be explicitly classified or rejected.



2\. What was actually built



A schema and enforcement tool that classifies counterfactual statements as accepted or rejected, with a mandatory, enumerated rejection reason.



3\. Why this matters in the real world



Auditors and regulators often encounter narratives like “the user should have…” or “the system could have…”. This artifact prevents such narratives unless they are institutionally plausible and role-appropriate.



4\. What went wrong or was unclear



Early counterfactual handling relied on human judgment rather than enforcement. This artifact corrected that gap.



5\. What I can now explain out loud



I can explain why certain “what-if” explanations are rejected and show the exact rule that rejected them.



6\. What remained unresolved



Counterfactual evaluation depends on the responsibility lattice being accurate.
